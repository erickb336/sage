- **You decide every takeover of a task between chiefs, in either direction.** In a shared logbook, each change of owning chief away from another chief now ends in one write: your answer to a takeover gate (T656).
  - `accept` of an offer no longer takes the task. It opens a takeover gate from the owning chief to the recipient. Your answer replace gives the task; keep changes nothing.
  - `claim` refuses a task that another chief released last, and names the takeover gate. The chief that released a task claims it back. After the move, a chief claims the tasks that its own provider started; a task from the other provider's logbook needs the takeover gate.
  - `gate replace`, now called the takeover gate in the guides, also takes a task with no owning chief.
  - `task-adopt` refuses in a shared logbook. An old, separate logbook keeps it as before.
  - `merge-check --chief C<n>` judges for that chief: a live task of another chief refuses. A Codex chief's `merge_check` passes its chief by itself.
  - One ledger: a verdict counts on its commit, whichever chief recorded it. A Claude chief that took a task from a Codex chief needs one Claude cycle on the head after your answer, on a `sage-pr import`, then merges with `sage-pr merge`. A Codex chief that took a Claude task counts the Claude cycles, and you merge in GitHub.
  - The branch keeps its name, and the PR its number. When another worktree holds the branch, the answer's line prints one safe command that frees it (`git switch --detach`, never `--force`).
  - The board's recommendation on a takeover gate after an accept says that the owning chief offered the task. Inferred work on an open PR now reads the owning chief's provider, not the `codex/` prefix of the branch.
