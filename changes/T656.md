- **You decide every takeover of a task between chiefs, in either direction.** In a shared logbook, each change of owning chief away from another chief now ends in one write: your answer to a takeover gate (T656).
  - `accept` of an offer no longer takes the task. It opens a takeover gate from the owning chief to the recipient. Your answer approve gives the task. Your answer decline ends the offer, and the owning chief keeps the task. A second accept, or a second `gate replace` of the same task and chiefs, prints the open gate and opens no other.
  - The takeover gate asks in the design's words, for example "Hand T331 and PR 92 from C2 (codex) to C1 (claude)?", with the options approve and decline. It names the runs that end as failed, and says that the branch keeps its name, the PR its number and the verdicts stay. A gate of an older sage, with replace and keep, still answers.
  - `claim` refuses a task that another chief released last, and names the takeover gate. The chief that released a task claims it back, and the line of `release` says so. After the move, a chief claims the tasks that its own provider started; a task from the other provider's logbook needs the takeover gate.
  - `gate replace`, now called the takeover gate in the guides, also takes a task with no owning chief. Its question names the chief that released the task last, or the logbook that the move brought it from.
  - `task-adopt` refuses in a shared logbook. An old, separate logbook keeps it as before.
  - `merge-check --chief C<n>` judges for that chief: a live task of another chief refuses. A Codex chief's `merge_check` passes its chief by itself.
  - One ledger: a verdict counts on its commit, whichever chief recorded it. A Claude chief that took a task from a Codex chief needs one Claude cycle on the head after your answer, on a `sage-pr import`, then merges with `sage-pr merge`. That cycle counts only the verdicts of review and QA runs that started after your answer. The gate's question says so, and the board keeps the task out of Ready to merge until that cycle. A move between two chiefs of one provider needs no such cycle. A Codex chief that took a Claude task counts the Claude cycles, and you merge in GitHub.
  - The branch keeps its name, and the PR its number. When another worktree holds the branch, the answer's line prints one safe command that frees it on a line of its own (`git -C '<worktree>' switch --detach`, with the path quoted, never `--force`), then says to check out the branch. A path with a control character gets no command.
  - The board: a takeover gate reads "Hand T331 and PR 92 from Codex · C2 to Claude · C1?". Its recommendation after an accept says that the owning chief offered the task. With no run that ends, it says so in place of "when you know that the chief stopped", and the offer line says that the recipient asks to take it and names the gate. Inferred work on an open PR now reads the owning chief's provider, not the `codex/` prefix of the branch.
